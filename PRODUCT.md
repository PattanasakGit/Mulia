# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People sharing a listening room: most often an office where one machine is wired to a speaker and colleagues drop in from their own laptops and phones to queue songs, pause, or skip; sometimes friends in different places listening together. They glance at the page for seconds at a time, usually one-handed, often on a phone. Thai-speaking. No accounts: a name and an animal avatar are the whole identity.

## Product Purpose

MULIA keeps one shared state for a room (which song, playing or paused, from which second) while every device plays YouTube itself. One device is the room's speaker; the others are remotes that can queue, pause, skip, seek, and optionally listen along. Success is a room where music never stops by accident, nobody's laptop starts blaring by surprise, and nobody has to learn the system.

## Brand Personality

Calm, plain, like good audio gear. Quiet paper-and-gold surface, the record and the play button are the stars. Voice is short and Thai, say little. Status is carried by small icons and states, not sentences.

## Anti-references

- Long strips of text under the play button (the first speaker bar).
- A control panel look: rows of small pill buttons that look unfinished.
- Colour or glow that pulls the eye away from the record and the song title.
- Gradient text, glassmorphism, identical card grids, side-stripe accents.

## Design Principles

1. The record and the transport are the room; everything else is a whisper beside them.
2. An icon says it, a label confirms it only when the meaning is not obvious. Never a sentence where a glyph will do.
3. Show the current state, hide the rarely used actions one tap away.
4. A remote must feel as capable as the speaker, and must never make sound by accident.
5. Quiet by default: no new colour, motion, or weight unless the state changed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard operable, visible focus. Every icon-only control has an accessible name and a tooltip. Text contrast at least 4.5:1, icon contrast at least 3:1. State is never colour alone (shape or glyph changes too). Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
